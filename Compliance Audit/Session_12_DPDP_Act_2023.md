# Session 12: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDP Act 2023) – India

---

## 1. What is the DPDP Act 2023?

**DPDP Act** = Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023  
- **Enacted:** 11 August 2023 (received Presidential assent)  
- **Effective Date:** To be notified by Central Government (rules yet to be fully notified as of 2024)  
- **Replaces:** Supersedes the IT (SPDI) Rules 2011 in areas it covers  
- **Administered by:** **Data Protection Board of India (DPBI)**  
- **India's first comprehensive data protection law**

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** The DPDP Act 2023 was passed after **6 years of deliberation** and multiple draft versions (Personal Data Protection Bill 2018, 2019, 2021 were all withdrawn). The final Act is relatively **short (44 sections)** compared to GDPR's 99 articles.

---

## 2. Scope and Applicability

### Applies to:
- Processing of **digital personal data** within India
- Processing of digital personal data **outside India** if it relates to offering goods/services to individuals in India

### Does NOT apply to:
- Personal data processed for **personal/domestic purposes**
- Personal data made **publicly available** by the data principal themselves
- **Non-digital** personal data (offline, paper records — unless digitized)

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** Unlike GDPR, the DPDP Act **only covers digital personal data** — paper/offline data is outside its direct scope unless it is digitized. This is a major distinction.

---

## 3. Key Definitions Under DPDP Act 2023

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Personal Data** | Any data about an identifiable individual |
| **Digital Personal Data** | Personal data in digital form |
| **Data Principal** | The individual to whom personal data relates (equivalent to "Data Subject" in GDPR) |
| **Data Fiduciary** | Entity that determines purpose and means of processing (equivalent to "Data Controller") |
| **Data Processor** | Processes data on behalf of Data Fiduciary (same as GDPR processor) |
| **Consent Manager** | Registered entity enabling data principals to manage consent |
| **Processing** | Automated operations on personal data |
| **Significant Data Fiduciary** | Notified by Central Government based on volume/sensitivity of data |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:**  
> - **Data Principal** = GDPR's Data Subject (the individual)  
> - **Data Fiduciary** = GDPR's Data Controller (the organization)  
> - DPDP Act uses unique Indian terminology — exam questions love testing these distinctions

---

## 4. Grounds for Processing Personal Data

Unlike GDPR's 6 lawful bases, DPDP Act 2023 primarily recognizes **2 bases**:

### 4.1 Consent
- Must be **free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous** with a clear affirmative action
- Given through a **Consent Manager** or directly to Data Fiduciary
- **Consent notice** must be provided before seeking consent — must be clear, plain language

### 4.2 Legitimate Uses (Deemed Consent — no explicit consent required)
- Voluntary data sharing by data principal
- State and its instrumentalities for **subsidies, benefits, services, licenses, permits**
- **Medical emergencies** or epidemics
- Employment purposes
- **Public interest** (e.g., safety, judicial functions)

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** DPDP Act does NOT have "Legitimate Interests" as a separate ground like GDPR. Processing for state services and employment are covered under **"Legitimate Uses"** (deemed consent). This is simpler but less flexible than GDPR.

---

## 5. Obligations of Data Fiduciaries

Data Fiduciaries must:

1. **Provide a consent notice** — purpose, data to be collected, rights
2. **Implement reasonable security safeguards** to prevent data breaches
3. **Report data breaches** to DPBI and affected Data Principals in prescribed manner
4. **Erase data** when:
   - Purpose for which consent was given is fulfilled
   - Data Principal withdraws consent
5. **Ensure accuracy** of data used for significant decisions
6. **Appoint Data Protection Officer (DPO)** if designated as Significant Data Fiduciary
7. **Conduct Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA)** if significant data fiduciary
8. **Appoint Independent Data Auditor** if significant data fiduciary

---

## 6. Rights of Data Principals

| Right | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Right to Information** | Know what personal data is being processed |
| **Right to Correction and Erasure** | Correct inaccurate data; erase data no longer necessary |
| **Right to Grievance Redressal** | File complaints and have them resolved |
| **Right to Nominate** | Nominate another person to exercise rights in case of death/incapacity |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** DPDP Act gives **fewer rights** than GDPR. Notable absences: No explicit **Right to Data Portability**, no **Right to Object**, no explicit **Right to Restrict Processing**. The **Right to Nominate** is unique to India's DPDP Act (not in GDPR).

---

## 7. Duties of Data Principals

The DPDP Act uniquely imposes **duties on Data Principals** (individuals):
- Do not impersonate another person
- Do not suppress material information
- Do not register false/frivolous grievances or complaints

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** **GDPR has no duties/obligations on Data Subjects.** The DPDP Act is unique in imposing **duties on the individual** (Data Principal). Violations can attract penalties of up to **₹10,000**.

---

## 8. Children's Data (Age < 18)

- Data Principal below **18 years** = **Child**
- Processing children's data requires **verifiable parental consent**
- **Behavioral targeting/monitoring of children is prohibited**
- Data Fiduciary cannot track/profile children

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** GDPR sets the age of digital consent at **16** (default, but member states can lower to 13). India's DPDP Act sets it at **18** — higher than GDPR. Targeted advertising to children is **explicitly prohibited**.

---

## 9. Data Localization and Cross-Border Transfers

- Personal data can be transferred to **"trusted countries"** as notified by the Central Government (whitelist approach)
- **No blanket prohibition** on cross-border transfers (unlike some earlier draft bills)
- Significant Data Fiduciaries may face additional restrictions

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** The DPDP Act 2023 moved **away from data localization** requirements seen in earlier draft bills. Cross-border transfers are allowed to notified trusted countries, subject to Central Government restrictions.

---

## 10. Significant Data Fiduciaries (SDF)

Notified by Central Government based on:
- Volume and sensitivity of personal data processed
- Risk to rights of Data Principals
- Potential impact on sovereignty and integrity of India
- Risk to electoral democracy
- Security of the State
- Public order

**Additional obligations for SDFs:**
1. Appoint **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** — must be a person based in India
2. Appoint **Independent Data Auditor**
3. Conduct periodic **DPIAs**
4. Implement additional security measures

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** The concept of "Significant Data Fiduciary" is similar to GDPR's requirement for DPO for certain organizations, but it's **government-notified** (not self-determined). Major tech companies, social media platforms, and large data processors are expected to be notified as SDFs.

---

## 11. Data Protection Board of India (DPBI)

- **Regulatory body** under the DPDP Act
- Adjudicates complaints and breaches
- Can impose penalties
- Members appointed by Central Government
- Decisions can be appealed to **Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT)**

---

## 12. Penalties Under DPDP Act 2023

| Violation | Maximum Penalty |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Breach in observing obligations for children's data** | Up to **₹200 crore** |
| **Failure to take security safeguards causing breach** | Up to **₹250 crore** |
| **Failure to notify breach to DPBI/Data Principals** | Up to **₹200 crore** |
| **Non-compliance by Significant Data Fiduciary** | Up to **₹150 crore** |
| **Obstruction of DPBI** | Up to **₹50 crore** |
| **Breach of duties by Data Principal** | Up to **₹10,000** |
| **Other violations** | Up to **₹50 crore** |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** The **maximum penalty is ₹250 crore** (for security safeguard failure). This is **significantly lower** than GDPR's €20 million or 4% global turnover. However, penalties are per violation and can aggregate.

## 13. DPDP Act 2023 vs GDPR – Comparison

| Feature | DPDP Act 2023 | GDPR |
|---------|--------------|------|
| Country | India | EU |
| Covers | Only digital personal data | All personal data |
| Data Principal rights | 4 rights | 8+ rights |
| Lawful bases | 2 (Consent + Legitimate Uses) | 6 |
| Data portability | NOT explicitly provided | Yes |
| Age of child | 18 years | 16 years (default) |
| Max penalty | ₹250 crore (~€28M) | €20M or 4% global turnover |
| DPO | Only for SDFs | For qualifying organizations |
| Duties on individuals | YES (unique to India) | NO |
| Data localization | Whitelist approach | Adequacy decisions/SCCs |
| Regulatory body | DPBI | Supervisory Authorities |

## 14. Timeline of India's Data Protection Journey

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 2000 | IT Act 2000 enacted |
| 2011 | IT (SPDI) Rules under Section 43A |
| 2017 | **Justice Puttaswamy judgment** — Privacy is a fundamental right (Article 21) |
| 2018 | Justice Srikrishna Committee — Draft PDP Bill 2018 |
| 2019 | Personal Data Protection Bill 2019 introduced in Parliament |
| 2021 | JPC report; PDP Bill 2021 revised |
| 2022 | PDP Bill 2021 **withdrawn** by government |
| 2023 | **DPDP Act 2023 enacted** (August 11, 2023) |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** The **Puttaswamy Judgment (2017)** — full name *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India* — is the constitutional foundation for privacy as a fundamental right in India under **Article 21** (Right to Life and Personal Liberty). This judgment triggered the push for comprehensive data protection legislation.

## 15. Consent Manager

A new concept in DPDP Act 2023:
- **Consent Manager** = A registered entity acting as a **single point of contact** for Data Principals to manage consent across multiple Data Fiduciaries
- Must be registered with DPBI
- Interoperable platform for giving, withdrawing, and managing consent
