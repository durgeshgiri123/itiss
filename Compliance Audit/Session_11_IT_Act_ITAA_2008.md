# Session 11: Information Technology Act 2000 & IT (Amendment) Act 2008 (ITAA 2008)

---

## 1. What is the IT Act 2000?

**Information Technology Act, 2000** (IT Act 2000)  
- India's **primary legislation** governing electronic commerce, cybercrime, and digital transactions  
- **Enacted:** 9 June 2000 (came into force 17 October 2000)  
- **Based on:** UNCITRAL Model Law on Electronic Commerce (1996)  
- **Administered by:** **Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY)**  
- **Amended by:** **Information Technology (Amendment) Act, 2008** — came into force **27 October 2009**

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** IT Act 2000 was India's **first law on cybercrimes and e-commerce**. It gives **legal validity to electronic records and digital signatures**. ITAA 2008 significantly strengthened it.

---

## 2. Key Objectives of IT Act 2000

1. Grant legal recognition to **electronic records and digital signatures**
2. Facilitate **electronic filing** of documents with government agencies
3. Facilitate **electronic commerce**
4. Prevent **computer crimes** and unauthorized access
5. **Amend** the Indian Penal Code (IPC), Indian Evidence Act, Banker's Books Evidence Act, and RBI Act for digital context

---

## 3. Important Definitions (IT Act 2000)

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Computer** | Any electronic, magnetic, optical device capable of processing data |
| **Computer Network** | Interconnection of computers |
| **Computer System** | Device/collection of devices including input/output/storage units |
| **Electronic Record** | Data, record, or document in electronic form |
| **Digital Signature** | Authentication of electronic records by subscriber's private key |
| **Electronic Signature** | Broader term introduced in ITAA 2008 (includes biometrics, OTP, etc.) |
| **Cyber Café** | Any facility providing internet access to the public |
| **Intermediary** | Any entity that receives, stores, or transmits data on behalf of another |
| **Hacker** | Person who gains unauthorized access to a computer system |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** ITAA 2008 replaced **"Digital Signature"** with the broader **"Electronic Signature"** to include newer authentication technologies like OTP, biometrics, etc.

---

## 4. Key Provisions – IT Act 2000 (Original)

### Chapter II – Digital Signatures and Electronic Records (Sections 3–10A)
- **Section 3** – Authentication of electronic records using digital signature
- **Section 5** – Legal recognition of digital signatures
- **Section 6** – Use of digital signatures in government filings

### Chapter III – Electronic Governance (Sections 4–10A)
- **Section 4** – Legal recognition of electronic records
- **Section 7** – Retention of electronic records (5 years)

### Chapter VII – Digital Signature Certificates
- **Section 35** – Certifying Authorities (CA) to issue digital signature certificates
- **Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA)** – Regulates CAs in India

---

## 5. Offences Under IT Act 2000 (Original Sections)

| Section | Offence | Punishment |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| **Section 43** | Unauthorized access, damage to computer | **Civil liability** – up to ₹1 crore compensation |
| **Section 65** | Tampering with computer source documents | Imprisonment up to **3 years** OR fine up to **₹2 lakh** or both |
| **Section 66** | Computer-related offences (hacking) | Imprisonment up to **3 years** OR fine up to **₹5 lakh** or both |
| **Section 67** | Publishing obscene material in electronic form | Imprisonment up to **5 years** AND fine up to **₹1 lakh** |

---

## 6. ITAA 2008 – Major Additions & Changes

The **Information Technology (Amendment) Act 2008** made sweeping changes:

### 6.1 New/Amended Offences

| Section | Offence | Punishment |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| **Section 43A** (NEW) | Failure to protect sensitive personal data by body corporates | **Compensation** (no fixed cap — courts decide) |
| **Section 66A** | Sending offensive/menacing messages (struck down in 2015) | Was: Imprisonment up to **3 years** |
| **Section 66B** | Receiving stolen computer resources | Imprisonment up to **3 years** OR fine up to **₹1 lakh** |
| **Section 66C** | Identity theft (using others' electronic signature/password) | Imprisonment up to **3 years** AND fine up to **₹1 lakh** |
| **Section 66D** | Cheating by personation using computer | Imprisonment up to **3 years** AND fine up to **₹1 lakh** |
| **Section 66E** | Violation of privacy (capturing/transmitting private images) | Imprisonment up to **3 years** OR fine up to **₹2 lakh** |
| **Section 66F** | **Cyber Terrorism** | Imprisonment up to **life imprisonment** |
| **Section 67A** | Publishing sexually explicit material | Imprisonment up to **5 years** AND fine up to **₹10 lakh** |
| **Section 67B** | Child pornography | Imprisonment up to **5 years** AND fine up to **₹10 lakh** (first conviction) |
| **Section 67C** | Failure of intermediaries to preserve records | Imprisonment up to **3 years** AND fine |
| **Section 69** | Government power to intercept, monitor, decrypt | Powers given to government agencies |
| **Section 69A** | Blocking of websites by government | Power to block public access |
| **Section 69B** | Monitoring and collecting traffic data | By government |
| **Section 70** | Protected system designation | Imprisonment up to **10 years** |
| **Section 70B** | CERT-In as national nodal agency | CERT-In designated |
| **Section 72A** | Disclosure of information in breach of lawful contract | Imprisonment up to **3 years** AND fine up to **₹5 lakh** |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** **Section 66A was struck down** by the Supreme Court of India in **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)** as unconstitutional (violating freedom of speech under Article 19(1)(a)). This is a highly tested fact.

## 7. Section 43A – Body Corporate Liability (CRITICAL)

Section 43A requires **Body Corporates** (companies) that possess, deal, or handle any sensitive personal data to implement **reasonable security practices and procedures**.

**Rules under Section 43A:**  
The **IT (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011** specify:

### Sensitive Personal Data (SPDI) Includes:
1. Password
2. Financial information (bank account, card details)
3. Physical, physiological, and mental health condition
4. Sexual orientation
5. Medical records
6. Biometric information

### Reasonable Security Practices:
- Organizations must implement **ISO/IEC 27001** OR follow DSCI security standards  
- OR any other standard approved by Central Government

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** Under Section 43A rules, if a body corporate fails to implement reasonable security practices and this results in **wrongful loss or gain**, it must pay **compensation** to the affected person. The amount is not capped in the Act — it's determined by the Adjudicating Officer or court.

## 8. Intermediary Liability (Section 79)

**Intermediaries** include: ISPs, search engines, social media platforms, e-commerce sites, cloud providers.

**Safe Harbor (Section 79):** Intermediaries are **not liable** for third-party information hosted IF:
- They don't initiate, select, or modify the content
- They observe due diligence
- They **act on notice to remove** illegal content (notice-and-takedown)

**IT Intermediary Guidelines (2021):** Updated rules requiring large social media platforms (>5 million users) to:
- Appoint a **Chief Compliance Officer** (Indian resident)
- Appoint a **Nodal Contact Person**
- Appoint a **Resident Grievance Officer**
- Enable **traceability** of first originator of messages

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** The IT Intermediary Guidelines (2021) — officially "Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021" — created significant controversy regarding **end-to-end encryption and traceability**.

---

## 9. CERT-In (Section 70B)

**CERT-In** = Computer Emergency Response Team – India  
- Designated as the **national nodal agency** for cybersecurity incidents  
- Under **MeitY**  
- Issues advisories, handles incident coordination, coordinates with government and private sector

### CERT-In Directions 2022 (CRITICAL for exams):
- Organizations must report **cybersecurity incidents within 6 hours** of noticing them
- Must maintain logs for **180 days**
- Must synchronize ICT system clocks with **NTP (Network Time Protocol)** servers

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** CERT-In's 6-hour incident reporting requirement is **much stricter** than GDPR (72 hours) or HIPAA (60 days). Exam questions test this difference.

---

## 10. Cyber Appellate Tribunal (CAT)

- Established under the IT Act
- Handles **appeals** against orders of Adjudicating Officers
- **Adjudicating Officer** – Resolves civil disputes under IT Act (up to ₹5 crore jurisdiction)
- For amounts above ₹5 crore — parties go to **High Courts**

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** The IT Act established **Adjudicating Officers** for civil matters and the **Cyber Appellate Tribunal** for appeals. Criminal matters go to regular courts.

---

## 11. Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA)

- Appointed under IT Act 2000
- **Licenses and regulates Certifying Authorities (CAs)** that issue Digital Certificates
- India's **Root CA**
- Major licensed CAs in India: **eMudhra, NSDL, SafeScrypt, (n)Code Solutions**
