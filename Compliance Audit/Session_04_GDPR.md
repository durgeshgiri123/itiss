# Session 4: General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

---

## 1. What is GDPR?

**GDPR** = General Data Protection Regulation  
- **Enacted by:** European Union (EU)  
- **Effective Date:** **25 May 2018** (adopted in April 2016, enforcement from May 2018)  
- **Replaces:** EU Data Protection Directive 95/46/EC  
- **Scope:** Applies to any organization **worldwide** that processes personal data of EU residents

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** GDPR has **extra-territorial scope** — a company in India or the USA that handles EU citizens' data must comply with GDPR. The regulation protects EU **residents**, not just EU citizens.

---

## 2. Key GDPR Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Personal Data** | Any information relating to an identified or identifiable person |
| **Data Subject** | The individual whose data is being processed |
| **Data Controller** | Decides the purpose and means of processing personal data |
| **Data Processor** | Processes data on behalf of the controller |
| **Processing** | Any operation on personal data (collect, store, use, share, delete) |
| **Consent** | Freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous indication of agreement |
| **Special Categories** | Sensitive data requiring extra protection |
| **Pseudonymization** | Replacing identifying fields with artificial identifiers |
| **Anonymization** | Data processed to irreversibly prevent identification |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:**  
> - **Controller vs Processor**: A hospital (controller) hires a cloud provider (processor). If a breach occurs, **both can be liable** under GDPR.  
> - **Pseudonymized data is still personal data** under GDPR.  
> - **Anonymized data is NOT personal data** under GDPR.

---

## 3. The 7 GDPR Principles (Article 5)

```
1. LAWFULNESS, FAIRNESS, AND TRANSPARENCY
2. PURPOSE LIMITATION
3. DATA MINIMISATION
4. ACCURACY
5. STORAGE LIMITATION
6. INTEGRITY AND CONFIDENTIALITY (Security)
7. ACCOUNTABILITY
```

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** A common exam question asks how many principles GDPR has — the answer is **7**. Memorize acronym: **L-P-D-A-S-I-A**

| # | Principle | Meaning |
|---|-----------|---------|
| 1 | **Lawfulness, Fairness, Transparency** | Process lawfully; tell subjects what you're doing |
| 2 | **Purpose Limitation** | Collect for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes only |
| 3 | **Data Minimisation** | Collect only what is necessary |
| 4 | **Accuracy** | Keep data accurate and up to date |
| 5 | **Storage Limitation** | Don't keep data longer than necessary |
| 6 | **Integrity & Confidentiality** | Protect data with appropriate security |
| 7 | **Accountability** | Controller is responsible and must demonstrate compliance |

---

## 4. Lawful Bases for Processing (Article 6)

There are **6 lawful bases**:

1. **Consent** – Clear, affirmative consent given by the data subject
2. **Contract** – Processing necessary for contract performance
3. **Legal Obligation** – Processing required by law
4. **Vital Interests** – To protect someone's life
5. **Public Task** – Official authority or public interest function
6. **Legitimate Interests** – Necessary for genuine interests (balanced against subject's rights)

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** **Consent** must be **freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous**. Pre-ticked boxes do NOT count as valid consent under GDPR. Silence or inactivity is not consent.

---

## 5. Special Categories of Data (Article 9)

These require **explicit consent** or other strong basis:
- Racial or ethnic origin
- Political opinions
- Religious or philosophical beliefs
- Trade union membership
- **Genetic data**
- **Biometric data** (when used for identification)
- Health data
- Sex life or sexual orientation

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** **Genetic and biometric data** were newly classified as special categories under GDPR (they were not in the old Directive). Questions often test this.

---

## 6. Data Subject Rights (Articles 12–23)

| Right | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Right to Be Informed** | Know what data is collected and how it's used |
| **Right of Access** | Request a copy of their data (Subject Access Request - SAR) |
| **Right to Rectification** | Correct inaccurate data |
| **Right to Erasure** ("Right to be Forgotten") | Request deletion of data |
| **Right to Restrict Processing** | Limit how data is used |
| **Right to Data Portability** | Receive data in machine-readable format |
| **Right to Object** | Object to processing (including profiling) |
| **Rights re: Automated Decision-Making** | Not be subject to solely automated decisions |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** 
> - The **"Right to be Forgotten"** = **Right to Erasure** (Article 17) — it is NOT absolute. Exceptions apply (e.g., legal obligation to retain data).
> - **Data Portability** applies only when processing is based on **consent or contract**.

---

## 7. Data Breach Notification Requirements

- **72-hour rule** – Organizations must notify the **supervisory authority** within **72 hours** of becoming aware of a breach (if it poses risk to individuals)
- Must also notify **affected individuals** without undue delay if the breach is likely to result in **high risk** to their rights and freedoms
- **Documentation** – All breaches must be documented, even if not reported

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** The 72-hour clock starts when the organization **becomes aware** of the breach, not when the breach actually occurred. Organizations must maintain a **breach register** regardless of whether they notify authorities.

---

## 8. Data Protection Officer (DPO)

**When is DPO mandatory?**
1. **Public authorities** or bodies
2. Organizations carrying out **large-scale regular/systematic monitoring** of individuals
3. Organizations processing **special categories of data** at large scale

**DPO responsibilities:**
- Inform and advise on GDPR obligations
- Monitor compliance
- Cooperate with supervisory authority
- Act as contact point for data subjects and authority
- Must be **independent** — cannot be instructed by the organization

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** A DPO **cannot** be fired or penalized for performing their duties. The DPO can be **internal or external**. The DPO does NOT have personal liability for GDPR breaches — the **controller** is liable.

---

## 9. Privacy by Design and Default (Article 25)

- **Privacy by Design** – Privacy must be embedded into the design of systems from the outset
- **Privacy by Default** – The most privacy-protective settings must be the default

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** Privacy by Design was originally a concept by **Ann Cavoukian** (Canadian Privacy Commissioner) before GDPR adopted it as a legal requirement.

---

## 10. Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)

Required when processing is **likely to result in high risk** to individuals, especially:
- Large-scale processing of special categories
- Systematic profiling
- Large-scale monitoring of public areas

**DPIA Process:**
1. Describe the nature, scope, context of processing
2. Assess necessity and proportionality
3. Identify and assess risks
4. Identify mitigation measures

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** A DPIA is **required before** processing begins, not after. If risk cannot be reduced, you must consult the **supervisory authority** before proceeding.

---

## 11. International Data Transfers

Personal data can only be transferred outside the EU/EEA if:
- **Adequacy decision** – EU Commission has decided the country provides adequate protection
- **Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)** – Approved contract terms
- **Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs)** – For intra-group transfers
- **Certifications** – Approved certification mechanisms

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** The **EU-US Privacy Shield** was **invalidated** in 2020 (Schrems II case). A new framework — **EU-US Data Privacy Framework** — was adopted in **2023**. SCCs remain the most common mechanism.

---

## 12. GDPR Penalties (Article 83)

Two tiers:

| Tier | Applies To | Maximum Fine |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Lower tier** | Basic obligations (DPO, breach notification, DPIA) | €10M or 2% of global annual turnover |
| **Upper tier** | Core principles, data subject rights, lawful transfers | **€20M or 4% of global annual turnover** |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** Always state **whichever is higher** — not "up to" a fixed amount. Google was fined €50M in 2019 by France's CNIL for GDPR violations.

---

## 13. Supervisory Authorities

- Each EU member state has a **Supervisory Authority (SA)** to enforce GDPR
- UK has **ICO** (Information Commissioner's Office) post-Brexit
- Ireland's **DPC** (Data Protection Commission) oversees major US tech companies with EU HQ in Ireland
- **Lead Supervisory Authority (LSA)** handles cross-border cases (one-stop-shop mechanism)

---

## 14. GDPR vs UK GDPR

After Brexit (2021), the UK retained a version called **UK GDPR** — largely mirrors EU GDPR but enforced by **ICO**. The EU granted the UK an **adequacy decision** in 2021.
