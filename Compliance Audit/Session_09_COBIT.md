# Session 9: COBIT Framework

---

## 1. What is COBIT?

**COBIT** = Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies  
- **Developed by:** **ISACA** (Information Systems Audit and Control Association)  
- **Current Version:** **COBIT 2019** (latest as of exams; COBIT 5 still referenced in older exams)  
- **Purpose:** IT **governance and management** framework — aligns IT with business goals and ensures value delivery, risk management, and resource optimization  
- **Type:** Voluntary framework; widely used for IT governance, audit, and compliance

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** COBIT is an **IT Governance** framework, not purely a cybersecurity framework. It focuses on how IT should be governed and managed across the entire enterprise. Compare: **NIST CSF** = cybersecurity risk; **COBIT** = IT governance + management.

---

## 2. COBIT Evolution

| Version | Year | Key Change |
|---------|------|-----------|
| COBIT 1 | 1996 | Audit focus |
| COBIT 2 | 1998 | Control focus |
| COBIT 3 | 2000 | Management focus |
| COBIT 4.0/4.1 | 2005/2007 | IT governance focus |
| COBIT 5 | 2012 | Business-focused governance, 5 principles |
| COBIT 2019 | 2018/2019 | Flexible design factors, 6 principles |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** **COBIT 5 = 5 principles, 7 enablers**. **COBIT 2019 = 6 principles, 40 governance/management objectives**. Know which version the exam targets.

---

## 3. COBIT 2019 – 6 Principles

```
1. Provide Stakeholder Value
2. Holistic Approach
3. Dynamic Governance System
4. Governance Distinct from Management
5. Tailored to Enterprise Needs
6. End-to-End Governance System
```

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** The most important principle to remember: **"Governance Distinct from Management"** — Governance is the board/executive level (WHAT and WHY); Management is the operational level (HOW).

---

## 4. COBIT 5 – 5 Principles (for older exam questions)

```
1. Meeting Stakeholder Needs
2. Covering the Enterprise End-to-End
3. Applying a Single Integrated Framework
4. Enabling a Holistic Approach
5. Separating Governance from Management
```

---

## 5. Governance vs Management in COBIT

| | Governance | Management |
|-|-----------|-----------|
| **Who** | Board, executives | Management, operational staff |
| **Focus** | Evaluate, Direct, Monitor (EDM) | Plan, Build, Run, Monitor (PBRM) |
| **Question** | WHAT should be done? WHY? | HOW is it done? |
| **COBIT Domain** | EDM domain | APO, BAI, DSS, MEA domains |

---

## 6. COBIT 2019 – Core Model (Governance & Management Objectives)

### Governance Domain:
**EDM** – Evaluate, Direct, Monitor (5 objectives)

### Management Domains (4 domains):
| Domain | Full Name | Focus |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| **APO** | Align, Plan, Organize | Strategic alignment, risk, architecture |
| **BAI** | Build, Acquire, Implement | Projects, change management |
| **DSS** | Deliver, Service, Support | Operations, incident management, business continuity |
| **MEA** | Monitor, Evaluate, Assure | Performance, compliance, audit |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** **EDM = Governance** (5 objectives). **APO + BAI + DSS + MEA = Management** (35 objectives). Total = **40 objectives** in COBIT 2019.

---

## 7. COBIT 5 – 7 Enablers (Holistic Approach)

Factors that individually and collectively influence whether something will work:

```
1. Principles, Policies, and Frameworks
2. Processes
3. Organizational Structures
4. Culture, Ethics, and Behavior
5. Information
6. Services, Infrastructure, and Applications
7. People, Skills, and Competencies
```

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** COBIT 2019 replaces "enablers" with **"components"** but exams still test the **7 enablers** from COBIT 5.

---

## 8. COBIT 2019 – Performance Management (Capability Levels)

COBIT 2019 uses a **capability level** system:

| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **0 – Incomplete** | Process not implemented or fails to achieve purpose |
| **1 – Performed** | Process achieves its purpose |
| **2 – Managed** | Process is planned, monitored, and adjusted |
| **3 – Defined** | Process defined using a standard |
| **4 – Quantitatively Managed** | Process measured with quantitative data |
| **5 – Optimizing** | Continuous improvement based on data |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** COBIT's capability levels are derived from the **CMMI (Capability Maturity Model Integration)** and **ISO/IEC 33000** series. Level 1 = basic; Level 5 = best-in-class optimization.

---

## 9. COBIT Design Factors

COBIT 2019 introduced **11 Design Factors** that influence the governance system:

1. Enterprise strategy
2. Enterprise goals
3. Risk profile
4. IT-related issues
5. Threat landscape
6. Compliance requirements
7. Role of IT
8. Sourcing model
9. IT implementation methods
10. Technology adoption strategy
11. Enterprise size

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** Design Factors are what make COBIT 2019 more **flexible and tailored** than COBIT 5. Organizations customize their governance system based on these factors.

---

## 10. COBIT and Other Frameworks

COBIT is designed to **integrate with other frameworks**:

| Framework | Integration with COBIT |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **ITIL** | COBIT provides governance; ITIL provides IT service management detail |
| **ISO 27001** | COBIT maps to ISO 27001 controls |
| **NIST CSF** | COBIT can be mapped to NIST CSF functions |
| **COSO** | COBIT integrates with COSO for enterprise risk management |
| **TOGAF** | Enterprise architecture alignment |

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** **COBIT + ITIL** is a very common pairing. COBIT = What/Why (governance); ITIL = How (operational service management). They are **complementary, not competing**.

---

## 11. Focus Areas in COBIT 2019

COBIT 2019 introduced **Focus Areas** — specific topics requiring extra attention:

1. **Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs)**
2. **Cybersecurity**
3. **Digital Transformation**
4. **Cloud Computing**
5. **Data Governance**
6. **DevOps**
7. **Privacy**

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** Focus Areas replace what COBIT 5 called "professional guides." The cybersecurity focus area maps COBIT 2019 objectives to cybersecurity best practices.

---

## 12. ISACA – The Organization Behind COBIT

- **ISACA** = Information Systems Audit and Control Association
- Also publishes certifications: **CISA** (Certified Information Systems Auditor), **CISM** (Certified Information Security Manager), **CRISC** (Certified in Risk and Information Systems Control)

> 🔑 **Tricky Exam Tip:** 
> - **CISA** = Auditing focus  
> - **CISM** = Management/governance focus  
> - **CRISC** = Risk and IS controls focus  
> - All are ISACA certifications, closely aligned with COBIT
